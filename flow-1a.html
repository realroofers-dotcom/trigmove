The rules that keep it straight

What each site must never do

Warrant Wire never explains a filing. It says where the paper is. The moment it starts telling people what terms mean, nobody pays 8K10Q.
Triggered Short never sells. It hands people across. It carries his position and his filer number, and a page that argues and sells at the same time is worth less at both.
8K10Q never publishes a verdict on a company. It reads documents on request. Findings and suspects belong on Triggered Short, under his byline.
No company is called anything until the exhibit is read. The 8-K said “customary adjustments”; the exhibit held a blocker and a contra-revenue clause. That is the whole business in one sentence.

What is not built

The flow above works up to the point where somebody pays.
  • Accounts — no login anywhere. The ten-a-month tier needs a counter per email address, and a subscription needs somebody to belong to
  • Stripe — every price on every page, and no way to take a card
  • tests.html — the ten tests are a table pulled from a database. The site's actual argument has no page
  • suspects.html — the list of companies that failed, as its own page
  • The reading itself — 8K10Q takes a ticker and has nothing behind it yet
