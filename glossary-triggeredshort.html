Glossary

Every term we use, with the rule behind it, what it does not mean, and exactly what we count.

This is the definition. The reading is at 8K10Q.com — type a ticker there and somebody goes through the filing and the exhibit and tells you, in plain English, which of these terms are actually in it and what they do to a shareholder.

How to read an entry

Five fields, and the last one is the important one

Each entry gives the phrase as it appears in the filing, the rule or accounting standard that defines it, the plain-English meaning, the limits of that meaning, and the rule we apply when we count it.

That last field exists because an interpretation can be argued with and a measurement cannot. If a company or its auditors say we have read something wrong, the counting rule is published here in advance, applied identically to every company, and open to anyone who wants to reproduce it. Disagree with the rule and say so — we will publish the objection. But the rule was not invented for any particular company after the fact.

Where a figure is the company's own, we say so and we do not recompute it. Where a figure is ours, the method is here.

Deemed dividend

As it appears in the filinga deemed dividend of approximately $X was recorded

AuthorityASC 470-20 and ASC 260-10 (earnings per share available to common stockholders).

Plain EnglishWhen a company gives one group of holders something worth more than what they gave up, the excess is not an expense — it is treated as a payment to those holders. It reduces the earnings figure attributable to everyone else.

What it does not meanIt does not mean cash left the company, and it does not mean anyone broke a rule. It is a measurement of value transferred, made by the company and blessed by its auditors.

What we countWe print the company's own deemed dividend figure and the accession number of the filing it appears in. We never compute one ourselves.

Warrant inducement

As it appears in the filinginducement offer letter agreement

AuthorityNot an accounting term. It is a transaction, disclosed under Item 1.01 of Form 8-K with the agreement filed as an exhibit.

Plain EnglishThe company lowers the exercise price on warrants already outstanding to get the holders to exercise now, and usually issues new warrants as part of the deal.

What it does not meanIt does not mean the repricing was improper. Companies do this to raise cash when no other financing is available, and it is disclosed.

What we countWe count a filing as an inducement only when the agreement itself is filed as an exhibit. A press mention is not enough.

Beneficial ownership limitation (the blocker)

As it appears in the filingthe Holder shall not have the right to exercise any portion of this Warrant to the extent that... the Holder would beneficially own in excess of 4.99%

AuthorityBeneficial ownership is defined by Exchange Act Rule 13d-3. The cap itself is a contract term, not a rule.

Plain EnglishA ceiling written into the warrant that stops the holder from crossing a set percentage at any one moment. Holders exercise up to the line, sell down, and exercise again.

What it does not meanIt does not mean the holder can only ever own 4.99% of the company. It caps the position at a point in time, not the total number of shares that can pass through their hands.

What we countWe record the percentage as written in the exhibit and note whether the agreement allows the holder to raise it on notice.

Pre-funded warrant

As it appears in the filingpre-funded warrants with an exercise price of $0.0001

AuthorityDisclosed in the prospectus or purchase agreement; the form of warrant is filed as an exhibit.

Plain EnglishA warrant where nearly the whole purchase price is paid up front and the exercise price is a fraction of a cent. Economically it is a share, structured so the holder does not have to hold it as one yet.

What it does not meanIt is not a discount instrument by itself, and it is a normal way to accommodate a holder who cannot exceed a threshold.

What we countWe count these separately from priced warrants, because they represent shares that are effectively already sold.

Floor price

As it appears in the filingthe Exercise Price shall not be reduced below the Floor Price

AuthorityA contract term in the purchase agreement or warrant, usually set to satisfy an exchange listing rule.

Plain EnglishThe lowest level to which a resetting exercise price is allowed to fall.

What it does not meanA floor is a protection, not a red flag. The presence of one means there is a reset mechanism — that is what makes it worth recording.

What we countWe record the floor and, separately, whether a reset exists at all. The reset is the structure; the floor is its limit.

Variable rate transaction

As it appears in the filingthe Company shall not effect or enter into an agreement to effect any Variable Rate Transaction

AuthorityDefined inside the securities purchase agreement itself. Read the definition in that agreement; they differ.

Plain EnglishA financing whose conversion or exercise price moves with the market price rather than being fixed. Investors in an earlier deal often prohibit the company from doing another one.

What it does not meanThe phrase usually appears in a prohibition, not a permission. Finding it does not mean the company did one.

What we countWe record whether the phrase appears as a restriction the company accepted or as a description of the deal being done, and we say which.

Minimum Price

As it appears in the filinga price less than the Minimum Price

AuthorityNYSE American Company Guide §713(a); Nasdaq Rule 5635(d). Each exchange publishes its own definition.

Plain EnglishThe exchange's reference price for deciding whether a share issuance is at a discount. It is the lower of the official closing price and the average closing price over the prior five trading days — which means the prior day's close alone does not settle it.

What it does not meanA deal priced below yesterday's close is not automatically below the Minimum Price. Getting this backwards is the fastest way to be wrong in print.

What we countWe compute the five-day average ourselves from published closes and print both it and the company's stated figure. Where they differ we print the difference and attribute the company's number to the company.

Baby shelf (one-third rule)

As it appears in the filingGeneral Instruction I.B.6 of Form S-3

AuthorityForm S-3, General Instruction I.B.6.

Plain EnglishA company whose public float is under $75 million may sell no more than one third of that float in any twelve months on a shelf registration.

What it does not meanIt is not a ban on raising money. It caps the cheap instrument, which is why the expensive one gets used.

What we countWe pull the float figure stated on the cover of the company's own 10-K or 10-Q rather than computing one.

Non-accelerated filer

As it appears in the filingNon-accelerated filer ☒

AuthorityExchange Act Rule 12b-2 for the definition; Sarbanes-Oxley Section 404(b) for what it exempts.

Plain EnglishA smaller reporting company is not required to have an outside auditor attest to its internal controls over financial reporting. Management still certifies them.

What it does not meanIt is not a finding of weak controls. It means no independent party was required to check them.

What we countWe read the checkbox on page one of the annual report and record whether an attestation report appears. We never infer it.

Authorized vs outstanding

As it appears in the filing350,000,000 shares of common stock authorized... 45,892,668 shares issued and outstanding

AuthorityStated on the cover of every 10-K and 10-Q and in the charter.

Plain EnglishAuthorized is the ceiling the charter permits. Outstanding is what exists today. The gap between them is how many shares can still be issued without asking anybody.

What it does not meanA large gap is not wrongdoing. Every company keeps headroom.

What we countWe record both numbers at every reporting date and track movements in the ceiling separately from movements in the count.

Reverse split without a shareholder vote

As it appears in the filingpursuant to the laws of the State of Nevada... was not required to obtain stockholder approval

AuthorityNRS 78.207 (Nevada); DGCL 242(d)(2) (Delaware, as amended 2023).

Plain EnglishNevada permits a board to change the authorized shares and the issued shares in the same proportion without asking holders. Delaware requires a vote, but since 2023 only a majority of votes actually cast.

What it does not meanDoing this is lawful and often forced by an exchange listing requirement. Saying so is not an accusation.

What we countWe record the statute cited by the company, whether a vote occurred, and whether the authorized ceiling moved proportionally — then whether it moved back.

Going concern

As it appears in the filingsubstantial doubt about the Company's ability to continue as a going concern

AuthorityASC 205-40; disclosed in the financial statement notes and often in the audit opinion.

Plain EnglishManagement or the auditor has concluded there is substantial doubt the company can fund itself for the next twelve months.

What it does not meanIt is not a prediction of bankruptcy, and many companies carry it for years.

What we countWe record the first period the language appears, whether it has been removed since, and what the company said had changed.

Fails to deliver

As it appears in the filingCNS fails-to-deliver data

AuthorityRegulation SHO; the SEC publishes the data twice monthly.

Plain EnglishShares that did not change hands on the settlement date. The SEC publishes a count by security by date.

What it does not meanThe SEC states plainly that fails are not necessarily the result of short selling and are not evidence of naked short selling. The data names no broker and is aggregate by design.

What we countWe report frequency — the share of settlement dates with any fail — not share counts, because a reverse split makes raw counts incomparable across time.

Contra revenue

As it appears in the filingconsideration payable to a customer

AuthorityASC 606-10-32-25.

Plain EnglishWhen a company gives something of value to a customer, the value is subtracted from revenue rather than booked as an expense. A warrant issued to a customer can work this way.

What it does not meanIt is correct accounting, not a trick. It does mean the headline revenue figure already has the discount netted out of it.

What we countWe record whether the agreement says the warrant's value reduces the amounts paid for the goods, and who controls the valuation.

Item 4.02 (non-reliance)

As it appears in the filingNon-Reliance on Previously Issued Financial Statements

AuthorityForm 8-K, Item 4.02.

Plain EnglishThe company is telling investors that financial statements it already published should no longer be relied on.

What it does not meanIt is not automatically fraud. Restatements happen for technical reasons.

What we countWe check whether an Item 4.02 was ever filed. If none exists, we print that too — the absence is an answer.
